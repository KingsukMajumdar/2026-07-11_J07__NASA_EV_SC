\paragraph{Sensitivity to degradation parameter $\alpha_{\mathrm{comm}}$}
Table~\ref{tab:alpha_sens} presents the sensitivity of TAC
and BESS state of health to $\alpha_{\mathrm{comm}}$ over
a $\pm$30\% range around the calibrated value
($4.354\times10^{-5}$). A 30\% increase raises TAC by
0.639\%, while a 30\% reduction lowers it by
0.642\%, confirming that the two-stage
re-calibration assumption has limited impact on the
framework conclusions. The low sensitivity arises because
degradation cost constitutes a minor fraction of total TAC,
which is dominated by grid energy and demand charges.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to sub-hourly peak factor $k_{\mathrm{p}}$}
Table~\ref{tab:kp_sens} shows the effect of varying
$k_{\mathrm{p}}$ from 2.0 to 3.5.
The total TAC variation across this range is
3.932\%, confirming framework robustness to
uncertainty in sub-hourly peak characterisation.
At $k_{\mathrm{p}}=2.0$, TAC changes by -0.009\%
relative to the base; at $k_{\mathrm{p}}=3.5$,
TAC changes by +3.923\%.
Higher $k_{\mathrm{p}}$ increases grid demand charges
through larger unmitigated sub-hourly spikes, while
simultaneously increasing SC utilisation and BESS cycle
reduction benefit.
